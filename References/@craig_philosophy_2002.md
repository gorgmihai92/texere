Hegel's philosophy differ from Plato's in the following tow ways:
1. Plato's ideas were [[Universals|universals]] from which things and events in the world take their shapes and natures, but Hegel's doctrine embodies a sequence of events in which ideas are linked one of the other until the whole system is revealed.
2. In Platonian perspective, ideas exist independently of anything else, but in Hegel's perspectives ideas exists only if something embodies it. 
On the second point, we might say that Plato and Hegel stated a dual representation of ideas — Plato was an idealist (ideas are non-material, therefore these exist independently), and Hegel was a materialist (ideas exist just if something embodies them).

We can have abstract thinking, but ideally, our thoughts must take shape in the form of something physically. In this way, we can look at it, analize, draw conclusions and understand ourselves in a better way.
