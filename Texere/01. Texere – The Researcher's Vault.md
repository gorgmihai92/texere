*Ghidul este disponibil și în limba română: [[02. Texere – Vault-ul cercetătorului|Texere: Vault-ul cercetătorului]].*

Researchers usually work across several apps (Zotero, Word, Google Docs, LibreOffice), and their documents end up scattered across laptops, phones, Google Drive, hard drives and USB sticks.

**Texere** brings everything into one place, in Obsidian: references, notes, ideas, drafts and the final paper. This guide shows how to manage your sources, save key passages, write, and export your paper in the format you need. It applies to any kind of academic research; the examples come from philosophy.



**Is it worth changing the way you do research?**

Traditional research is a fractured experience: each stage happens in a different app, and only your memory holds them together. The difference, stage by stage:

![[Without and with Obsidian.png]]

| Stage                    | Without Obsidian                                                            | With Obsidian                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Finding a source         | You save it in Zotero, in the browser or in a downloads folder.             | You import it by DOI or ISBN, and the note and the PDF appear in the vault automatically.    |
| Reading                  | You underline the PDF, and your notes end up on paper, in Word or elsewhere. | You highlight the PDF, and the passage lands in the source's note, with the page and a link back to the text. |
| Connecting ideas         | You remember what each author said and where you wrote it down.             | Notes link to each other: you see every source on a concept, a theory or an author.          |
| Finding a quote again    | You search through PDFs, notebooks and folders.                             | You search for a word or a tag (e.g. `#disagree`) and find the passage with its page.        |
| Writing                  | You copy quotes from one app to another.                                    | You write in the same app that holds your notes and sources.                                 |
| Citing                   | You format every citation and the bibliography by hand, then check the style. | You write `[@citekey, p. 34]`, and citations and the bibliography are generated in the style you choose. |
| Submitting               | You paste the text into Word and redo the formatting.                       | You export straight to Word or PDF.                                                          |
| **Places you work in**   | **6: Zotero, browser, PDFs, Word, folders, notebook**                       | **1: the vault**                                                                             |

Once set up, your readings, notes and quotes stay connected, from your first seminar to your BA, MA or PhD.

**Step 1. Set up the vault (once)**

1. Install **Obsidian** (free) from [obsidian.md/download](https://obsidian.md/download).
2. Copy the whole vault folder to your laptop (not just the notes inside it). In Obsidian, choose **Open folder as vault** and select the folder.
3. When asked, choose **Trust author and enable plugins**. If you missed it: **Settings → Community plugins → Turn on community plugins**.
4. The setup starts by itself. A window shows what it does automatically and tells you when it's your turn: you only choose the language (English or Romanian) and where exported files are saved.
5. Read the **03. Texere – Setup report** note (the newest report comes first) and begin with **Start here**. If the report also has a **What you need to do** section (for example, installing Pandoc or LaTeX), follow the steps there, then run the setup again.

> [!tip] Something stopped working?
> Run the setup again: **Ctrl/Cmd + P** → **Texere: Setup**. It restores the settings and tells you what's missing. If that doesn't help (for example on Windows, where it hasn't been tested yet), see [[#8. Troubleshooting]].


**Step 2. Work.** Go to the stage you need:

1. **Add a source** → [[#3. Managing references]]
2. **Read and highlight** the key passages → [[#4. Reading and literature notes]]
3. **Write and cite** → [[#5. Writing academic papers]]
4. **Build the bibliography and export** to Word or PDF → [[#6. Bibliography and export]]

**Have a question?** Click the **Help** button (the life buoy in the left sidebar) or **Ctrl/Cmd + P** → **Texere: Help**, and type your question in your own words. You get a short answer and the matching passage from this guide.

## 1. Introduction

Obsidian is a note-taking app that works with folders on your own computer. Your notes form a network, not just a hierarchy of folders. The essentials:

- **Plain text:** notes are Markdown files (.md), which open in other apps too.
- **Local-first:** your data stays on your devices, under your control.
- **Links between notes:** notes point to each other, like Wikipedia pages. This is Obsidian's most important feature.
- **Extensibility:** plugins adapt the app to the way you work.

The Obsidian interface is explained in [this video guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z4AbijUCoKU).


### 1.1 Terms used in this guide

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Alias** | An alternative name for a note, so you can find it however it's spelled (e.g. the note Daoism has the alias Taoism). |
| **Backlinks** | The panel that shows every note linking to the open note. |
| **BibLib** | The plugin that imports sources by DOI, ISBN or arXiv and creates the literature notes and the bibliography. |
| **Bibliography** (`bibliography.json`) | The file in the **biblib** folder, generated by BibLib, from which Pandoc takes the source data when exporting. |
| **Citation** | A reference to a source in the text, written `[@citekey]`. On export it becomes, for example, (Craig, 2002). |
| **Citekey** | A source's unique code: author, a word from the title, and year (e.g. `craig_philosophy_2002`). |
| **Command palette** | The list of all commands, opened with **Ctrl + P** (Windows) or **Cmd + P** (Mac). |
| **CSL** | The file that sets the citation style (APA, Chicago etc.). In this vault it's `biblib/apa.csl`. |
| **DOI, ISBN, arXiv** | Unique identifiers for articles (DOI), books (ISBN) and preprints (arXiv), which BibLib uses to find a source's data. |
| **Extra Pandoc arguments** | The extra export settings: where the bibliography is, which citation style is used, and which program creates the PDF. |
| **Graph View** | A visual map of your notes and the links between them, opened from the left sidebar. |
| **LaTeX** (MacTeX, MiKTeX) | The typesetting program Pandoc uses to export to PDF. |
| **Link** | A reference to another note, written `[[Note name]]`. |
| **Literature note** | A source's note, in the **References** folder: the source's data, the attached PDF and the highlighted passages. |
| **Markdown** (.md) | The plain-text format notes are saved in. |
| **Note** | A text file in the vault, with a title, properties and content. |
| **Pandoc** | The program that turns notes into Word, PDF or other formats. |
| **PDF++** | The plugin you use to highlight passages in a PDF and copy them into notes, with a link to the page. |
| **Plugin** | An extension that adds new features to Obsidian (e.g. BibLib, PDF++, Pandoc Plugin). |
| **Properties** | The fields at the top of a note (e.g. **branch**, **attachment**, **title**) that describe it and link it to other notes. |
| **Tag** | A word preceded by `#` (e.g. `#fact`) that you can use to search and group notes or passages. |
| **Template** | A model note in the **Templates** folder that you duplicate as a starting point. |
| **Vault** | The folder where Obsidian keeps all your notes, files and settings, including the hidden `.obsidian` folder. |
| **Zotero Connector** | The browser extension that sends a page's data (article, book) straight to Obsidian. |

## 2. Vault structure

The vault has a few folders, each with its own role:

| Folder                                 | What it holds                                     | Can you change it?                         |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **References**                         | one note per source (book, article); the first 5 are examples | Yes, you can write in the notes, but don't rename them |
| **Documents**                          | the sources' PDFs                                 | Yes, you can add files                     |
| **Texere** | this guide (in English and Romanian), the **03. Texere – Setup report** note (created by the setup), the export test note and the research-methodology notes (in Romanian) | Yes |
| **Templates** | templates for papers and authors, plus the setup script **New laptop setup** | You can add templates, but don't delete or rename **New laptop setup** |
| **Scripts** | the scripts that start by themselves: the **Help** button and folding the guide's chapters | No |
| **Domains**                            | the [[Philosophy]] note, with the field's structure | Yes                                      |
| **Export** | exported files, if you chose it during setup | Yes |
| **Media**                              | images used in notes                              | No                                         |
| **biblib** and the **citekeylist** file | the bibliography, generated automatically        | No                                         |

The **Branch**, **Subdomains**, **Theories**, **Concepts**, **Movements** and **Historical Periods** folders hold one note, with a short definition, for each item in [[Philosophy]]. Note names are in English, and each note also has a Romanian alias: type `[[Etică` and Obsidian suggests the **Ethics** note.

<font color="#d83931">Important:</font> Don't rename, move or delete the **Media**, **biblib**, **References**, **Scripts** and **Templates** folders or the **citekeylist** file. In the **References** notes, don't change the **id** and **citation-key** properties. Every other file can be edited or deleted.

> [!warning] If you accidentally deleted or changed **Scripts** or **Templates**
> Without them, the automatic setup, the **Help** button and chapter folding don't work. Copy the missing folder from the original vault (the copy you first received), restart Obsidian and run the setup again.


**The Philosophy note**

The [[Philosophy]] note holds the field's structure: branches, subdomains, historical periods, movements, theories, concepts and authors.

- When you fill in a note's properties, use the values from [[Philosophy]]: that way notes link to each other and information is easy to find. Example: the note [[@vica_constantin_avatarobots_2026]] has [[Ethics]] as its **branch**. Open the [[Ethics]] note and the **Backlinks** panel shows every source in this branch.
- You can see the links between notes in **Graph View**.
- The [[Philosophy]] note can be edited, duplicated and restructured for other fields. Need a map for another field? Write to [hi@georgemihai.com](mailto:hi@georgemihai.com).





## 3. Managing references

Each source gets a note in the **References** folder, named after its citekey (e.g. `@craig_philosophy_2002`). The source's PDF is saved in **Documents**.

> [!example] The example sources
> The 5 sources in **References** (Blackburn, Constantinescu, Craig, Dummett, Vică) are examples, kept to show what a literature note looks like. After setup you can delete them, along with their PDFs in **Documents**. If you delete them, also replace the example citations in the [[New article]] template.

You can add a source in two ways.

### 3.1 From Obsidian (the simplest)

1. Press **Ctrl/Cmd + P** and search for **BibLib: Create literature note**.
2. Enter the identifier (DOI, ISBN, arXiv etc.) and click **Lookup**. Obsidian fills in the source's data automatically.
	![[Screenshot 2026-10-15 at 12.45.01.png]]
3. (optional) Attach a file. If the PDF isn't attached automatically, you can add it later (see [[#3.3 Attaching a PDF]]).
	![[Screenshot 2026-10-15 at 12.48.16.png]]

For sources without an identifier (e.g. old editions), fill in the data by hand in the same window.

### 3.2 From the browser, with Zotero Connector

1. Install [Zotero Connector](https://www.zotero.org/download/) for your browser.
2. Go to the page of [an article](https://policyreview.info/articles/analysis/avatarobots-democracy-metaverse).
3. Click **Zotero Connector** in the browser, with **Obsidian** open and the Zotero app closed.

> [!warning]- Not all of the article's data comes through?
> 1. In Obsidian, turn off Zotero Connector in **Settings → BibLib → Zotero Integration → Enable Zotero Connector**.
> 2. In the browser, open the extension's settings: **Zotero Connector → Options / Preferences → Advanced → Translators**.
> 3. Click **Reset Translators** and wait for the confirmation.
> 4. With the BibLib integration still off, reload [the article's page](https://policyreview.info/articles/analysis/avatarobots-democracy-metaverse).
> 5. Turn **Enable Zotero Connector** back on in Obsidian.
> 6. Try the import from the browser again.

### 3.3 Attaching a PDF

If the reference has no attachment:
1. Drag the PDF into the **Documents** folder.
2. Open the reference's note (e.g. [[@craig_philosophy_2002]]).
3. In the **attachment** property, type `[[`, search for the file and select it.





## 4. Reading and literature notes

You open the PDF from the source's note, and you save the key passages in the same note.

1. Open the note (e.g. [[@craig_philosophy_2002]]) and, in the **attachment** property, click the PDF's title.
	![[Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 09.18.45.png]]
2. Select a passage and choose a color from the **PDF++** palette (see the table below).
3. Paste the passage into the literature note (**Ctrl + V** or **Cmd + V**).

Each color marks a type of idea, so you can easily structure arguments and notes:

| Color | Tag | What you mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yellow | `#fact` | the author's claims and theses |
| red | `#disagree` | ideas you disagree with |
| green | `#agree` | ideas you agree with |
| blue | `#connection` | links to other texts, authors or ideas |
| purple | `#question` | questions the text raises for you |
| orange | `#definition` | definitions |
| grey | `#word` | terms to remember |

![[Screenshot 2026-10-16 at 15.37.26.png]]

Each saved passage starts with the color's tag and ends with a link `@citekey, p.4`, which takes you straight to the page in the PDF. Example: the note [[@vica_constantin_avatarobots_2026]].

> [!example] How it works, from a real note
> In [[@craig_philosophy_2002]], a highlighted passage with the reader's own comment underneath:
> ```
> #fact In fact philosophy is extremely hard to avoid, even with a conscious effort. @craig_philosophy_2002, p.12
> Even sceptics who said that philosophy is useless have a certain set of values…
> ```
> 1. **Highlight** the passage in the PDF (yellow = `#fact`) and paste it into the note: the link takes you back to page 12.
> 2. **Comment** in your own words, right under the passage.
> 3. **Write:** the first paragraph of [[04. Texere – Export test]] starts from this passage and cites it as `[@craig_philosophy_2002, p. 12]`.
> 4. **Export:** the result is [[04. Texere – Export test.pdf]], in the **Export** folder.

> [!tip] Tip
> - To gather ideas of the same type from all your readings, search for the tag (e.g. `tag:#disagree` for ideas you disagree with).
> - The literature note already has the sections **Thesis**, **Arguments**, **Counterarguments** and **Conclusions**. After reading, briefly note the author's thesis, the arguments and your objections.






## 5. Writing academic papers

### 5.1 Start from a template (optional)

Duplicate the [[New article]] template (or make your own), rename the copy and fill in the **title** property. The template already has the sections of a paper and, at the end, the heading **Bibliography**: leave it empty, the bibliography appears under it on export.

### 5.2 Citing sources

- A citation looks like [@vica_constantin_avatarobots_2026]: `@` followed by the citekey, in square brackets.
- You'll find the citekey in [[citekeylist]] or in the note's name in **References**.

How citations appear on export (APA style):

| You write in the text | Appears in the document |
| --- | --- |
| `[@craig_philosophy_2002]` | (Craig, 2002) |
| `[@craig_philosophy_2002, p. 34]` | (Craig, 2002, p. 34) |
| `[@craig_philosophy_2002, pp. 34-36]` | (Craig, 2002, pp. 34–36) |
| `[@craig_philosophy_2002; @dummett_origins_1996]` | (Craig, 2002; Dummett, 1996) |
| `@craig_philosophy_2002 [p. 34]` | Craig (2002, p. 34) |
| `[see @craig_philosophy_2002, ch. 2]` | (see Craig, 2002, ch. 2) |

When you quote or paraphrase, always give the page.

## 6. Bibliography and export

> [!attention] Before your first export
> Export works after the setup in **Step 1**. If it stops working, see [[#8. Troubleshooting]].

> [!tip] Try it first
> The note [[04. Texere – Export test]] cites the example sources. Export it as PDF (**Ctrl/Cmd + P** → **Pandoc Plugin: Export as PDF (via LaTeX)**): if the PDF appears in the **Export** folder with citations and a bibliography, everything works. Compare it with the example already exported: [[04. Texere – Export test.pdf]].

Every time you export:

1. **Build the bibliography:** **Ctrl/Cmd + P** → **BibLib: Build bibliography**. BibLib updates [[citekeylist]] with all sources, so the document gets its citations and bibliography.
2. **Export the note:** **Ctrl/Cmd + P** → search for **Pandoc** and choose the format (Word .docx, PDF, LaTeX etc.).

## 8. Troubleshooting

First, run the setup again: **Ctrl/Cmd + P** → **Texere: Setup**. If you can't find the command: **Ctrl/Cmd + P** → **Templater: Create new note from template** → **New laptop setup** (the setup script). If that doesn't help either, follow the steps below.

### 8.1 Manual setup

Plugin settings keep the paths from the laptop the vault was created on. The automatic setup does steps 3 and 5 by itself; by hand:

1. **Copy the whole vault**, including the hidden `.obsidian` folder (plugins and settings). On a Mac, hidden folders show up in Finder with **Cmd + Shift + .** (period).
2. **Turn on the plugins:** **Trust author and enable plugins** or **Settings → Community plugins → Turn on community plugins**.
3. **Check the bibliography tag.** **Settings → BibLib → Literature note tag** must say `literature_note, #literature_note`. Run **BibLib: Build bibliography**: the number of entries must equal the number of notes in **References**.
4. **Install the programs for exporting papers:** [Pandoc](https://pandoc.org/installing.html) and, for PDF, [MacTeX](https://tug.org/mactex/) (Mac) or [MiKTeX](https://miktex.org/) (Windows).
5. **Fill in the export settings** in **Settings → Pandoc Plugin**:
	- **Output folder**: the folder where exported files appear. Empty means next to the note.
	- **Extra Pandoc arguments**: in the `--bibliography=` and `--csl=` lines, replace the part before `/biblib/` with your vault's location. Each line starts directly with `--`, with no spaces in front and no quotation marks.
	- **Windows only**: replace `--pdf-engine=/Library/TeX/texbin/xelatex` with `--pdf-engine=xelatex`.
6. **Check the font.** The font in the `mainfont` property must be installed on your laptop. The [[New article]] template uses Arial; if you don't have it, use Times New Roman or your preferred font.

> [!tip] How to find the vault's location
> In Obsidian, right-click the **biblib** folder → **Reveal in Finder** (Mac) or **Show in system explorer** (Windows). Then:
> - **Mac:** in Finder, hold **Option**, right-click the folder → **Copy "biblib" as Pathname**.
> - **Windows:** right-click the folder → **Copy as path**, then delete the quotation marks at the start and end.
>
> Paste the path after `--bibliography=` and add `/bibliography.json`, then after `--csl=` and add `/apa.csl`. Result:
> ```
> --bibliography=/Users/your-name/Documents/obsidian-research/biblib/bibliography.json
> --csl=/Users/your-name/Documents/obsidian-research/biblib/apa.csl
> ```

### 8.2 If an error appears

The steps in the table are those in [[#8.1 Manual setup]].

| What you see                                                        | Why                                                                             | What to do                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `citation ... not found`                                            | the bibliography wasn't built or the `--bibliography=` path points elsewhere    | steps 3 and 5                                                                                                                          |
| `Undefined control sequence \citeproc`                              | appears together with the error above                                           | goes away once the citations are found                                                                                                 |
| a message that Pandoc can't be found                                | Pandoc isn't installed or is somewhere else                                     | step 4; then put its location in **Pandoc path** (Mac: type `which pandoc` in Terminal; Windows: `Get-Command pandoc` in PowerShell)    |
| `xelatex not found`                                                 | LaTeX isn't installed or the `--pdf-engine=` path is from another system        | steps 4 and 5                                                                                                                          |
| `The font "..." cannot be found`                                    | the font in `mainfont` isn't installed                                          | step 6                                                                                                                                 |
| the exported file doesn't appear                                    | **Output folder** points to a folder that doesn't exist on your laptop          | step 5                                                                                                                                 |
| MiKTeX asks to install packages                                     | normal on the first PDF export                                                  | accept the installation                                                                                                                |
| BibLib builds fewer entries than there are notes                    | the notes' tag doesn't match the setting                                        | step 3                                                                                                                                 |
| the BibLib, Pandoc or PDF++ commands are missing                    | the plugins aren't turned on or the `.obsidian` folder is missing               | steps 1 and 2                                                                                                                          |
| the setup doesn't start by itself after **Trust author**            | the **Texere** plugin isn't turned on               | **Settings → Community plugins** → turn on **Texere**, then run the setup                                                   |
| the **Help** button or the **New laptop setup** script is missing | the **Scripts** or **Templates** folder was deleted, moved or renamed      | copy the folder from the original vault, restart Obsidian and run the setup again                                                      |
| Zotero Connector doesn't send the source to Obsidian                | the Zotero app is open                                                          | close Zotero                                                                                                                           |
| Obsidian is slow the first time it opens                            | the Smart Connections plugin is indexing your notes                             | wait a few minutes                                                                                                                     |





## 9. Frequently asked questions

1. **Can I change the proposed structure?**
	Yes, it's only a proposal. Some changes (settings, scripts) need technical knowledge or the help of an AI assistant.
2. **What's essential to keep if I want to organize my documents differently?**
	- Follow the steps in [[#3. Managing references]], [[#5.2 Citing sources]] and [[#6. Bibliography and export]], however you organize your notes.
	- Keep the folders marked "No" in [[#2. Vault structure]].
	- Don't change the **BibLib**, **Pandoc Plugin** or **PDF++** settings without technical knowledge, apart from those in [[#8.1 Manual setup]].
3. **Something stopped working (export, bibliography, plugins). What do I do?**
	See [[#8. Troubleshooting]].
4. **Can I use a citation style other than APA (for example, Chicago)?**
	Yes. Download the style from the [Zotero Style Repository](https://www.zotero.org/styles), save the .csl file in **biblib** and replace `apa.csl` with its name in **Extra Pandoc arguments**.
5. **I accidentally deleted or changed some text. Can I get it back?**
	Yes, from **Settings → File recovery**: Obsidian automatically keeps earlier versions of your notes.
6. **Which version do I have, and how do I update the vault?**
	The version and the changes are in [[#11. Version history]]. To update without losing your notes:
	1. Back up your vault (see [[#7. Backups]]).
	2. From the new vault, copy over yours: the guide, the **Scripts** and **Templates** folders, the hidden `.obsidian/plugins/texere` folder and anything else the new version mentions.
	3. **Don't** replace **References**, **Documents** or your own notes.
	4. Restart Obsidian; the setup starts by itself if needed.
