# Meditations on First Philosophy

## Abstract

Six meditations in which Descartes doubts everything that can be doubted, finds one certainty (that he exists as a thinking thing) and rebuilds knowledge from it: God exists and is no deceiver, so what we perceive clearly and distinctly is true; mind and body are distinct substances; the material world exists.

## Thesis

Knowledge needs a foundation no doubt can shake: the [[Cogito]], and a God who is no deceiver and guarantees our clear and distinct ideas.

## Arguments

- **Method of doubt** (Meditation I): the senses sometimes deceive, waking can't be told from dreaming, and an evil demon might deceive me even in mathematics.
- **The cogito** (Meditation II): "I am, I exist" is true each time I think it; I am a thinking thing.
- **The wax** (Meditation II): bodies, too, are known by the mind, not by the senses.
- **Trademark argument** (Meditation III): the idea of an infinite being can only come from an infinite being.
- **Ontological argument** (Meditation V): existence belongs to the essence of [[God]].
- **Real distinction** (Meditation VI): the mind is indivisible, the body divisible, so they are different substances ([[Dualism]]).

## Counterarguments

- **The Cartesian circle:** clear and distinct perception proves God, and God guarantees clear and distinct perception.
- **[[Immanuel Kant|Kant]]:** existence is not a real predicate (against the ontological argument).
- **Elisabeth of Bohemia:** how can a mind without extension move a body? (the [[Mind-Body Problem]])

## Conclusions

Modern [[Foundationalism]] and [[Rationalism]] start here, and so does the [[Mind-Body Problem]] in its modern form.

### Meditation I: Of the things of which we may doubt

#claim I had accepted, even from my youth, many false opinions for true [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 22] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=26&annotation=734R|PDF]]

The starting point: doubt everything once, to rebuild knowledge on firm ground. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 22]

#claim All that I have, up to this moment, accepted as possessed of the highest truth and certainty, I received either from or through the senses. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 23] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=27&annotation=736R|PDF]]

#agree it is the part of prudence not to place absolute confidence in that by which we have even once been deceived [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 23] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=27&annotation=738R|PDF]]

A reasonable rule, though a strict one: by it, almost nothing we know would survive. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 23]

#claim there exist no certain marks by which the state of waking can ever be distinguished from sleep [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 24] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=28&annotation=740R|PDF]]

The dream argument: it doubts the senses as a whole, not just a single perception. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 24]

#question some malignant demon, who is at once exceedingly potent and deceitful, has employed all his artifice to deceive me [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 26] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=30&annotation=742R|PDF]]

Does even mathematics survive this doubt? Descartes answers only in Meditation III, with [[God]]. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 26]

### Meditation II: Of the nature of the human mind

#connection Archimedes, that he might transport the entire globe from the place it occupied to another, demanded only a point that was firm and immovable [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 28] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=32&annotation=744R|PDF]]

The image behind [[Foundationalism|foundationalism]]: one certain point, and everything else built on it. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 28]

#claim I am, I exist, is necessarily true each time it is expressed by me, or conceived in my mind [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 29] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=33&annotation=746R|PDF]]

The [[Cogito|cogito]] as it appears here: not an inference (I think, therefore I am), but true each time I think it. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 29]

#definition I am therefore, precisely speaking, only a thinking thing, that is, a mind (mens sive animus), understanding, or reason [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 31] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=35&annotation=748R|PDF]]

#definition It is a thing that doubts, understands, [conceives], affirms, denies, wills, refuses; that imagines also, and perceives. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 32] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=36&annotation=750R|PDF]]

Thinking is wider than reasoning: it includes willing, imagining and perceiving. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 32]

#claim is simply an intuition (inspectio) of the mind [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 35] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=39&annotation=752R|PDF]]

The wax: even bodies are known by the mind, not by the senses. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 35]

### Meditation III: Of God, that he exists

#claim all that is very clearly and distinctly apprehended (conceived) is true [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 39] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=43&annotation=754R|PDF]]

The rule of clear and distinct perception. It needs God as a guarantee, and God is proved with it: the Cartesian circle. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 39]

#definition some appear to me to be innate, others adventitious, and others to be made by myself (factitious) [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 41] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=45&annotation=756R|PDF]]

#definition By the name God, I understand a substance infinite, [eternal, immutable], independent, all-knowing [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 47] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=51&annotation=758R|PDF]]

#disagree God, at my creation, implanted this idea in me, that it might serve, as it were, for the mark of the workman impressed on his work [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 52] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=56&annotation=760R|PDF]]

Why must the idea of something infinite have an infinite cause? The causal principle carries the whole argument, and it isn't argued for. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 52]

### Meditation IV: Of truth and error

#claim I do not restrain the will, which is of much wider range than the understanding, within the same limits [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 59] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=63&annotation=762R|PDF]]

Error comes from my will judging beyond what I understand, not from God. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 59]

### Meditation V: Of the essence of material things, and again of God

#disagree the existence can no more be separated from the essence of God, than the idea of a mountain from that of a valley [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 65] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=69&annotation=764R|PDF]]

The ontological argument. [[Immanuel Kant|Kant]]'s reply: existence is not a property that a concept can contain. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 65]

### Meditation VI: Of the existence of material things, and the real distinction between mind and body

#question I am not only lodged in my body as a pilot in a vessel, but that I am besides so intimately conjoined, and as it were intermixed with it [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 78] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=82&annotation=766R|PDF]]

If [[Mind-Body Problem|mind and body]] are two different substances, how can they be this closely joined? Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia pressed Descartes on exactly this. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 78]

#claim body, from its nature, is always divisible, and that mind is entirely indivisible [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 82] [[Descartes 1641 (2022) – Meditations on First Philosophy.pdf#page=86&annotation=768R|PDF]]

The argument from divisibility for [[Dualism|dualism]]. [@descartes_meditations_2022, p. 82]
