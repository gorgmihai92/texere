Philosophy is the systematic, critical study of fundamental questions about existence, [[Knowledge|knowledge]], value, reason, mind and language.

This note maps philosophy as a discipline. Its metadata lists every branch, subfield, period, movement, theory, concept and author in this map. Each has its own note, with a short definition, that links back here. Open **Graph view**, or the **Backlinks** of any note, to explore the connections.

## How the map is organised

| Metadata | What it contains | Folder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| branch | the main areas of philosophy, such as [[Ethics]] | Branches |
| subfield | specialised fields within a branch, such as [[Metaethics]] | Subfields |
| period | the main periods in the history of philosophy, such as [[Ancient Philosophy]] | Periods |
| movements | schools and traditions with a history, such as [[Stoicism]] | Movements |
| theories | positions that can be defended or rejected, such as [[Compatibilism]] | Theories |
| concepts | key ideas and problems, such as [[Justice]] or [[Consciousness]] | Concepts |
| authors | philosophers, such as [[Aristotle]] | Authors |

Use the same metadata in your reading notes (for example **branch:** [[Ethics]]); then the **Backlinks** of [[Ethics]] list everything you read in that branch.

Some "-isms" are both a historical movement and a position (for example, [[Empiricism]]). Here each appears only once, in the category where it is most often discussed.
