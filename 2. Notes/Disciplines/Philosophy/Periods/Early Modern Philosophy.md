The 17th and 18th centuries, from Descartes to Kant: [[Rationalism|rationalism]] and [[Empiricism|empiricism]], the new science, the [[Social Contract Theory|social contract]] and the [[Enlightenment]].

## Sub-periods
- **Age of Reason** (17th century): the search for certain foundations of [[Knowledge|knowledge]]: Bacon, Descartes, Hobbes, Pascal, Spinoza, Leibniz, Locke.
- **Enlightenment** (18th century): reason, science and [[Rights|rights]]: Berkeley, Hume, Voltaire, Rousseau, Smith, Burke, Bentham and Kant.

## Main themes
- [[Certainty]] and the foundations of knowledge
- Reason or experience as the source of knowledge
- The relation between [[Mind-Body Problem|mind and body]]
- The [[Social Contract Theory|social contract]] and the [[Legitimacy|legitimacy]] of the state
- The limits of reason (Kant)

- [[René Descartes|Descartes]], *Meditations on First Philosophy* (1641)
- [[Thomas Hobbes|Hobbes]], *Leviathan* (1651)
- [[Benedict de Spinoza|Spinoza]], *Ethics* (1677)
- [[John Locke|Locke]], *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689)
- [[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz|Leibniz]], *Monadology* (1714)
- [[David Hume|Hume]], *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739–1740)
- [[Jean-Jacques Rousseau|Rousseau]], *The Social Contract* (1762)
- [[Immanuel Kant|Kant]], *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781)
