Philosophy after Kant, from about 1800 to 1900: [[German Idealism|German idealism]], [[Utilitarianism|utilitarianism]], [[Positivism|positivism]], Marx, and the critics of systems and of religion.

## Sub-periods
- **German idealism and its heirs** (1800–1840s): Fichte, Schelling and Hegel; Schopenhauer.
- **After Hegel** (1840s–1900): Marx and Engels, Kierkegaard, Comte's positivism, Mill's utilitarianism and liberalism, Nietzsche, and the first [[Pragmatism|pragmatists]] in America.

## Main themes
- History, [[Dialectic|dialectic]] and progress
- Society, labour and [[Alienation|alienation]]
- The individual, faith and existence (Kierkegaard)
- Utility, [[Freedom|liberty]] and reform (Bentham, Mill)
- The critique of religion and traditional morality (Nietzsche)

## Key works
- [[G. W. F. Hegel|Hegel]], *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807)
- [[Arthur Schopenhauer|Schopenhauer]], *The World as Will and Representation* (1818)
- [[Søren Kierkegaard|Kierkegaard]], *Either/Or* (1843)
- [[Karl Marx|Marx]] and [[Friedrich Engels|Engels]], *The Communist Manifesto* (1848)
- [[John Stuart Mill|Mill]], *On Liberty* (1859)
- [[Friedrich Nietzsche|Nietzsche]], *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–1885)
