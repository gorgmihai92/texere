Philosophy in the Christian, Byzantine, Islamic and Jewish worlds, centred on the relationship between [[Faith and Reason|faith and reason]].

## Sub-periods
- **Early Middle Ages** (6th–10th century): Boethius, Byzantine thought, the preservation of Greek and Latin learning.
- **High Middle Ages** (11th–13th century): the rise of universities and [[Scholasticism]], the recovery of Aristotle through Islamic and Jewish philosophers (Avicenna, Averroes, Maimonides), Anselm, Abelard and Aquinas.
- **Late Middle Ages** (14th century): Duns Scotus and William of Ockham; [[Nominalism|nominalism]] and the growing separation of faith and reason.

## Main themes
- How do faith and reason relate?
- Can the existence of [[God]] be proved?
- The problem of [[Universals|universals]]: realism or nominalism?
- [[Essence and Existence|Essence and existence]]
- Why does evil exist if God is good, and are we free?
- [[Natural Law Theory|Natural law]] as the basis of morality and politics
