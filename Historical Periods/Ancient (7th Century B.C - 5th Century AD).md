From the first Greek philosophers to the end of antiquity: the [[Pre-Socratic|Pre-Socratics]], Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, and the Hellenistic and Roman schools, alongside the classical traditions of China and India.

## Sub-periods
- **Pre-Socratic** (7th–5th century BC): the search for the first principle ([[Arche|arche]]) of nature: the [[Milesian School|Milesians]], Heraclitus, Pythagoras, the [[Eleatic School|Eleatics]], the [[Atomism|atomists]].
- **Classical** (5th–4th century BC): the [[Sophism|Sophists]], Socrates, Plato and Aristotle in Athens.
- **Hellenistic** (323–31 BC): schools of the good life: [[Stoicism]], [[Epicureanism]], [[Skepticism|Scepticism]] and [[Cynicism]].
- **Roman and Late Antique** (1st century BC – 5th century AD): Roman Stoicism (Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius), Cicero, [[Neoplatonism]] (Plotinus).

## Main themes
- What is the first principle of all things?
- Being and change: is reality one and unchanging (Parmenides) or in constant flux (Heraclitus)?
- How should we live, and what is [[Virtue|virtue]]? (Socrates)
- What are the Forms, and how is [[Knowledge|knowledge]] possible? (Plato)
- Substance, causes and purpose in nature (Aristotle)
- How can we reach [[Happiness|happiness]] and tranquillity of mind?
