From Descartes to Nietzsche: [[Rationalism|rationalism]] and [[Empiricism|empiricism]], the [[Enlightenment]], Kant, [[German Idealism|German idealism]], and the great 19th-century systems and their critics.

## Sub-periods
- **Age of Reason** (17th century): the search for certain foundations of [[Knowledge|knowledge]]: Descartes, Hobbes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Locke.
- **Enlightenment** (18th century): reason, science and [[Rights|rights]]: Berkeley, Hume, Voltaire, Rousseau, Smith and Kant.
- **19th century** (1800–1900): German idealism (Hegel), [[Utilitarianism|utilitarianism]] (Bentham, Mill), [[Positivism|positivism]] (Comte), Marx, Kierkegaard, Schopenhauer and Nietzsche.

## Main themes
- [[Certainty]] and the foundations of knowledge
- Reason or experience as the source of knowledge
- The relation between [[Mind & Body|mind and body]]
- The [[Social Contract Theory|social contract]] and the [[Legitimacy|legitimacy]] of the state
- The limits of reason (Kant)
- History, [[Dialectic|dialectic]] and progress
- The critique of religion and traditional morality
