# A brief guide to writing the philosophy paper

## Key points

- Doing philosophy, not reporting opinions: clarify the exact question, then answer it with a clear, logically structured argument.
- Parts: a precise thesis early (no grand historical narrative), definitions, motivation, a roadmap, the target argument reconstructed in your own words, your argument, objections and replies, a brief conclusion.
- One strong argument rather than a “shotgun” of weak ones; raise the strongest objections, not straw men.
- Evidence in philosophy is argument: not authority, not dictionary definitions; empirical data only with its relevance explained.
- Quote sparingly and explain every quotation; use the first person freely; signpost often (“I will argue that…”, “My second objection is…”).
- Words with a technical sense: valid, sound, begs the question, vague.
