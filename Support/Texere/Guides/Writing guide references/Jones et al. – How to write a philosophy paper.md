# How to write a philosophy paper

## Key points

- Read actively and slowly; find the structure through indicator words (conclusion: therefore, thus, hence; reason: because, since; counterargument: some might argue).
- An argument is a conclusion plus premises; a belief or a feeling is not an argument. Each paragraph must advance the argument.
- The thesis sentence states the conclusion and previews the premises; premises should be ones a reasonable opponent accepts.
- Charity: present the strongest version of a view; explain why someone holds it, not only what they hold.
- Avoid “I believe / I think / I feel / in my opinion”, rhetorical questions in place of argument, purple prose, dictionary definitions of technical terms.
- A paper: clear thesis, explicit premises, a fair objection with a reply. B paper: vague thesis, hedged “I think”, objections listed but not developed. C paper: assertions without reasons.
