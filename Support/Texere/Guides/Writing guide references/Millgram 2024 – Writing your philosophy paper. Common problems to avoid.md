# Writing your philosophy paper: Common problems to avoid

## Key points

- One point, statable in one short sentence, told to the reader at the start.
- The argument is the backbone and the test: cut any paragraph you cannot justify as needed for it.
- A terse introduction with a roadmap; only as much exposition of others as your argument needs.
- Concrete, low-key examples; test every abstract term (can you say it without the jargon?); road signs throughout.
- Phrases to avoid include “delve”, “shed light on”, “some argue that” without a named instance, “begs the question” meaning “raises”.
